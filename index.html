🫧 How rare answers turn into depth

Read the qualifier first

Name an instrument that uses a double reed.
AnswerDoes it fit?
OboeYes. It uses a double reed.
ShawmYes. This older instrument also uses a double reed.
ClarinetIt uses a single reed, so it misses the condition.

Think of the oboe first? Keep it in mind while you look for a less familiar instrument that still meets the rule. With a few seconds left, go with the one you can finish typing.

A practice question written for this guide.

What one round can add

The game awards a preset rarity tier for each accepted answer. Each point adds 10 metres, so a Rare answer can take you six times as far as a Plankton answer.

A One in a Krillion answer earns 100 points, the most you can score on a prompt. The scoring guide covers the full scale and what happens when the timer runs out.

🪸 On the surface and below

🧭 What to try on your next dive

Compare one round with a friend

Two players can end at the same depth with different answers. Find a prompt where you split, then read the exact wording together. Did a qualifier rule one answer out? Did both go through at different tiers? The Krillion answers guide helps you make sense of the results after you play.

Practice with two ways in

For Krillion Geography, picture a country with several land borders and recall its neighbours clockwise. For Krillion Sports, take one sport and name the playing surface, then a piece of equipment. Starting from a detail gives you somewhere to go when your first answer is the one everyone thinks of.
